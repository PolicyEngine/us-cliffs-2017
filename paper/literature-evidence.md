## Boundaries on comparisons

No source in this table supplies a directly interchangeable national estimate of persons in households whose annual resources fall after the paper's exact +$1,000 counterfactual and health valuation. In particular:

- A mean or median rate does not identify the share above 100%.
- An EMTR of exactly 100% yields no gain; it does not imply a strict resource loss.
- Complete loss of one benefit need not exceed additional earnings.
- A conditional recipient rate cannot be multiplied by the entire population.
- Current-year and lifetime rates require separate labels.
- All facts in the comparison table are verified except the explicitly unconfirmed 2015 distributional perturbation.

The manuscript uses cliffs for adverse finite-change resource comparisons across taxes and benefits. That measure does not identify mathematical notches: the sign test in `cliffs2017/changes.py::moves_against` also admits continuous resource declines and rounding effects, as Section 3.3 explains. The tax literature supplies definitions and a behavioral comparison, not national incidence estimates for this experiment.
