Illustrative model outputs. We calculate 2025 annual resources for a 40-year-old single parent and a 4-year-old dependent in Harris County, Texas. The parent earns $30 per hour over 52 weeks; hours rise with earnings. The parent does not claim cash Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF). Resources exclude health benefits and include noncash food benefits through the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) and the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC). The [configuration](../examples/snap_model_boundary_zoom_2025.json) records rent, utilities, assets, coverage and other claiming assumptions. We calculate 61 points over $0–$60,000 and 201 points over $33,650–$33,850; lines join calculated points and break across the observed drop. Between $33,726 and $33,727 of earnings, we calculate annual resources falling $413.34 and annual SNAP falling from $597 to $183. USDA's tables set FY2025 limits for January–September and FY2026 limits for October–December in calendar 2025. We infer from these dates and annual totals that the FY2025 months lose SNAP at this jump and the three FY2026 months retain it; we did not calculate monthly amounts. We calculate annual SNAP of $159 at $34,000 and zero at $35,000 on the coarse grid, without locating the later change at $1 resolution. Texas's Health and Human Services Commission publishes a $2,811 monthly categorical gross-income limit for a two-person household in FY2025; PolicyEngine applies $2,810.50. This model illustration does not establish the exact Texas legal cutoff. [Texas HHSC Bulletin 24-12](https://fhb.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/mepd-and-twh-Bulletin-24-12.pdf#page=5) supplies the published 165% categorical limit. PolicyEngine 6.19.0 uses US 2.2.1, Core 3.33.3 and spm-calculator 1.0.0 for these calculations.
